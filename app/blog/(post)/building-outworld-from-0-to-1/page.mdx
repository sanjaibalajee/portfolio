# building outworld from 0 to 1

<PostMeta date="Oct 29, 2026" dateTime="2026-10-29" />

in may 2025, [tejas](https://tejassuds.com/) brought up an idea for an events platform inside fanpit. that became outworld. by october 25, 2026, <Mark>24 communities had listed 120 events</Mark> on it, including shows still to come.

i built most of it after hours, late at night and across weekends, with his direction and ideas. i wrote about those first few months in [sidequestmaxxing my way back](/blog/sidequestmaxxing-my-way-back).

building helps me cope when life gets a bit much. sometimes i just want to make something because it sounds fun. ai has made it easier to follow through on those ideas, and <Mark>i've been enjoying the work a lot lately</Mark>.

## the first events, may to december 2025

the first version ran on flask and mongodb, hosted on app runner. by the end of june, <Mark>someone could book, pay, receive a ticket and check in at the door</Mark>. the people running the event used a small ios app to scan tickets.

by september, communities needed seat maps, cohosts, waitlists and a way to reach their guests. <Mark tone="amber">i started seeing classmates book through outworld</Mark>, and communities came back to run another event. seeing that happen made me want to keep working on it.

## rebuilding it, march to june 2026

in early 2026, i rebuilt the organizer dashboard and the public event page.

in march and april, i moved event images from mongodb to s3 and cloudfront, with direct browser uploads. i copied the existing files over and <Mark tone="blue">kept the old urls working</Mark>.

in may, bolt, our ai agent, joined the dashboard to help set up and run events.

## keeping it running, july and august

the api ran on app runner, but background jobs and scheduled reminders lived on a server i maintained myself. logs filled the disk. an update could leave the api on one version and the jobs on another. <Mark tone="amber">sometimes two copies of the scheduler ran and sent the same reminder twice.</Mark>

aws had also [closed app runner to new customers on april 30, 2026](https://aws.amazon.com/apprunner/), with no new features planned. existing customers could keep using it, but i wanted to move before more of the system depended on it.

in july, i moved the api, background jobs and scheduler to ecs. they ran as separate services using the same version of the app, with one scheduler for reminders.

during the move, <Mark tone="amber">health checks passed even while people were getting 502s</Mark>. the app server was closing connections while the load balancer still expected to use them. changing the timeouts fixed it.

by august, i could follow one booking through the logs, from the api to the payment update and ticket email. i also fixed rate limiting that was <Mark tone="amber">treating vercel's shared address as the visitor's ip</Mark>.

i replaced redis cloud with valkey on aws, moving one service at a time. the old queue stayed available in case i needed to switch back.

### getting tickets to people

i moved ticket emails and whatsapp messages into a queue too. a booking no longer had to wait for the provider to send them, and <Mark>a failed message could retry on its own</Mark>. login codes still went out directly, without waiting behind event messages.

after that, i added alerts for failed email sends, separate from the checks on the worker itself.

invoices became a background job too, instead of being generated while handling a payment update. a retry reuses the same invoice. the files stay private, with signed links for downloads.

## checkout and recovery, september

the old checkout tried to reserve seats, call razorpay and start ticket delivery in one request. if razorpay created an order but the response never reached us, i couldn't tell from the timeout whether it had worked.

i split checkout into steps that could recover if something stopped halfway. a mongodb transaction saves the order and reserves its seats together, so <Mark>two buyers can't both get the last seat</Mark>. retrying the request reuses the same checkout instead of holding more seats.

a razorpay timeout now leaves the booking pending while a background job checks whether the order was created.

late payments needed a separate path. if the seat hold had already expired, the system tries to reserve the seats again. if they're gone, it queues a refund. <Mark tone="blue">receiving the same payment update twice can't confirm the booking twice.</Mark>

tickets, invoices and messages happen after the payment is recorded. the worker tracks completed jobs so a restart can pick up the unfinished work.

the tests now include two buyers reaching for the last seat, payment updates arriving twice and jobs stopping halfway through.

## the dashboard was getting slow, september

the analytics dashboard was <Mark tone="amber">loading raw posthog events into pandas to count them</Mark>. as the data grew, opening a report could keep the database and the app busy long enough to time out.

i moved the counting into postgres and built a smaller table with just the data the reports needed. it updates every fifteen minutes, adding new events instead of scanning the whole history each time. activity uploaded late by an offline phone gets picked up when it arrives.

report updates got their own queue, so a slow report wouldn't hold up a ticket or invoice. i kept the original data and the old way of reading it available while i compared the numbers. if they didn't match, i could switch back.

## tickets could change hands, october

in october, i added ticket transfers. communities choose which ticket types allow it. someone with a four-seat booking can <Mark>send two seats to a friend without giving up the other two</Mark>.

the original buyer keeps the order, invoice and any refund. the recipient gets the ticket and fills in their own registration details.

the sender confirms by email, and the recipient accepts with the invited address verified. accepting a whole-ticket transfer replaces the qr, so <Mark tone="blue">the old ticket stops working</Mark>.

the holder change and the new qr are saved together. check-in rejects a scan if the ticket changed while it was being checked.

while an invitation is pending, the seats it offers can't be offered to anyone else. invitations expire or close if the ticket is no longer eligible.

## back to swiftui, october 4 to 25

the expo app worked, but i never quite liked how it felt. i'd look at the controls, navigation and transitions and still want them to feel more like ios.

i've loved building with swiftui since college. putting a view together, running it and moving things around is still fun. i'd wanted to bring fanpit over for a while. on october 4, i started rewriting the whole app in native swiftui.

claude opus 5.5 helped port the existing app to swiftui. i spent most of my time adjusting the screens, running them and trying again until they felt right.

<Mark>thirteen days later</Mark>, on october 17, the native version of [Fanpit Live reached the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/fanpit-live/id6753718568). <Mark tone="amber">it got through review without a rejection this time</Mark>, which was a nice surprise after the original expo release.

by october 25, i'd made it iphone duo ready too. discovery, event details, tickets and the organizer screens all needed to work with the extra space. these are from that work in the simulator.

## why i keep building

i used to get excited about an idea, build the part i liked, and leave the rest for later. coding agents help me work through the parts i'd usually put off, so more of those ideas get finished now.

watching these agents get better has changed what feels worth trying in a free evening. i can hand over a chunk of work, have it run tests, and come back to something i can try. there's still plenty of reading, fixing and changing my mind. i enjoy that part too. <Mark tone="blue">fewer ideas end at "i'll get to it someday."</Mark>

in march 2016, sam altman wondered how many people would have fallen in love with an ai chatbot by 2026.

it feels like a good time to be an engineer. think of something, open a terminal and see how far you can get. <Mark tone="amber">we get to have some fun with this.</Mark>

<Mark>i'm happier when i'm making things.</Mark> sometimes it's a feature someone will use, sometimes it's a small app i wanted for myself. <Mark tone="blue">ai has made building a bigger part of my life lately</Mark>, and i'm grateful for that.
